\section{Experiments}
\label{sec:experiments}
The experiments address four research questions. \textbf{RQ1}: Does \method{} change answer quality on Web and other evidence formats? \textbf{RQ2}: How do the trigger, score normalization, modality routing, and EAB each contribute? \textbf{RQ3}: How do EAB and the other evidence-use operations affect order sensitivity, citation support, and dependence on the emphasized chunks? \textbf{RQ4}: How robust is the controller to retrieval errors, calibration, and the backbone, and what does it cost? RQ2 examines Challenge~1 through the trigger, normalization, and routing controls, and RQ3 examines Challenge~2.

\subsection{Experimental Setup}
\label{sec:setup}
\textit{Datasets and evidence.} WebQA~\cite{chang2022webqa} is our primary Web task: its textual questions require facts retrieved from a global pool of about \ph{} text snippets and \ph{} images, each of which is one chunk. Calibration and development questions come from the WebQA training split, so every one of the 4{,}966 validation questions is an evaluation question. We report all of them as WebQA (full val) and report separately the 2{,}511 questions whose gold facts are images (\webimg{}); the remaining 2{,}455 have text gold facts. Three further benchmarks vary the evidence format. OK-VQA~\cite{marino2019okvqa} tests image-based knowledge with passages retrieved from the GS training corpus of 112{,}724 sentences (GS-112K)~\cite{luo2021weakly}; the corpus was chosen on development questions among three candidates (Appendix~\ref{app:protocol}). HybridQA~\cite{chen2020hybridqa} asks multi-hop questions over Web tables and the passages linked from their cells. VideoRAG-Synth asks about instructional procedures shown in HowTo100M videos~\cite{miech2019howto100m}, following VideoRAG~\cite{jeong2025videorag}; each retrieved video or clip is one chunk of sampled frames and its transcript. Only OK-VQA supplies context, the question image; the other benchmarks start cold, with text and image candidates on WebQA, table and text candidates on HybridQA, and video and clip candidates on VideoRAG-Synth. For HybridQA and VideoRAG-Synth we use the query sets of the UniversalRAG suite~\cite{yeo2026universalrag} together with subsets of its corpora: passages and tables from HybridQA only (\ph{} passages and \ph{} tables) and the \ph{} videos that remained downloadable (Appendix~\ref{app:protocol}). Every method reads the same candidate pool, the top five per modality returned by the suite's own retrievers (Appendix~\ref{app:protocol}).

\textit{Splits.} Each dataset is shuffled with a fixed seed (2027) and split into calibration, development, and evaluation questions that are disjoint by construction, and the evaluation set does not depend on the calibration size. On OK-VQA the 5{,}046 validation questions give 500 calibration questions, 256 development questions, and $5{,}046-500-256=4{,}290$ evaluation questions. On HybridQA the 2{,}000 queries give 500 calibration questions, 256 development questions, and $2{,}000-500-256=1{,}244$ evaluation questions. On VideoRAG-Synth the 374 queries give 100 calibration questions, 50 development questions, and $374-100-50=224$ evaluation questions. On WebQA, \ph{} calibration questions and the 256 development questions are drawn from the training split.

\textit{Evaluation metrics.} WebQA scores separate answer accuracy from BARTScore-based fluency (Appendix~\ref{app:webqa_scoring}); OK-VQA uses the official VQA accuracy; HybridQA and VideoRAG-Synth use the metrics of the UniversalRAG suite, exact match (EM) and ROUGE-L, with F1 and BERTScore in Appendix~\ref{app:supp}. Relative improvement over a baseline is $(\mathrm{ours}-\mathrm{baseline})/\mathrm{baseline}\times100\%$; all other differences are absolute points. For order sensitivity, we fix the candidate chunks of each question and apply 14 orderings of the five retrieval ranks, identically to all questions: the relevance-sorted and reverse-sorted packings, and 12 random permutations drawn with three seeds. The orderings are run on the first 1{,}000 evaluation questions of each dataset (all 224 for VideoRAG-Synth). We report the standard deviation of the score, the gap between relevance-sorted and reverse-sorted packing, and the flip rate, the share of questions whose correctness changes; a question is correct when its official score is at least 50. We also report the attention--relevance divergence (ARD); attribution precision (AP) and Faith of citations, where alias hits and NLI judgments are reported separately (Appendix~\ref{app:protocol}); AURC and the Spearman $\rho$ between uncertainty and error; and the wrong-route (WR) and coverage (Cov.) rates of routing.

\textit{Baselines.} The baselines separate gains from retrieving evidence, from deciding when and where to retrieve, and from changing how the retrieved chunks are used. Within a dataset, all arms are decoded in the same run with the same question, candidate pool, backbone, prompt, decoding limits, and at most $k=5$ retrieved chunks. (1) Direct answering without retrieval. (2) Static concatenation (\texttt{static\_joint}): the joint top-$k$ over all modalities, ranked by calibrated relevance and packed in that order, without a probe round, routing, or bias. Cross-encoder + static concatenation uses bge-reranker-v2-m3~\cite{chen2024m3}, built on BGE-M3, to select the chunks for the same concatenation; Found-in-the-Middle attention calibration~\cite{hsieh2024found} is applied to the same joint input. (3) M2-RAG~\cite{ma2025m2rag} receives the same joint top-$k$ chunks and the question image. Its official single-stage summarizer writes a document from the chunks, and an answer extractor that we added produces the short answer; M2-RAG uses its official chat template, and its image pixel budget equals that of the other arms (Appendix~\ref{app:protocol}). It does not accept video. (4) SeaKR~\cite{yao2025seakr}, which reads internal states on the same joint input, with its threshold set on the calibration queries to the retrieval rate of $\Uomni$; it is run on all evaluation questions. (5) Modality routing: UniversalRAG~\cite{yeo2026universalrag}, whose official router, trained after removing our evaluation questions from its training data, decides from the query text alone whether to retrieve and which of its corpora to search, with clip and video as separate modalities. A query whose labels match no corpus is answered directly and counted, and its candidates are ranked by the suite's own retrieval scores. Omni-Aware and its ablations use their own routed input (Section~\ref{sec:routing}). Next-token entropy (NTE) as the trigger and a PASTA-inspired uniform bias~\cite{zhang2024pasta} are ablations of our controller (Section~\ref{sec:ablation}); the NTE threshold is the calibrated quantile that matches the retrieval rate of $\Uomni$. The baselines use the default settings of their official implementations, except for the SeaKR threshold and the FiTM temperature (Appendix~\ref{app:protocol}). Unlike the controller, they received no development-set tuning, so the comparison is not budget-matched.

\textit{Prompts and answer format.} All configurations receive identical instructions, and every chunk carries an identifier such as \texttt{[E3]} for citation. OK-VQA asks for a word or phrase, WebQA for one sentence, and HybridQA and VideoRAG-Synth keep the answer instructions of the UniversalRAG suite (the exact answer, and a complete sentence). The first answer token alone determines \ph\% of OK-VQA, \ph\% of HybridQA, \ph\% of VideoRAG-Synth, and \ph\% of WebQA answers, which is why EAB starts at the row that predicts it (Section~\ref{sec:eab}).

\textit{Implementation and calibration.} Qwen3-VL-8B-Instruct~\cite{bai2025qwen3vl} is the primary backbone, with InternVL3.5-8B~\cite{wang2025internvl35} for transfer. Text and image retrieval use BGE-large-en-v1.5~\cite{xiao2024cpack} and CLIP ViT-L/14~\cite{radford2021clip} with FAISS~\cite{johnson2021faiss}; on HybridQA and VideoRAG-Synth these encoders score the shared candidates to obtain $r_j$. Tables are serialized row by row, and each retrieved video or clip is represented by 8 uniformly sampled frames with its transcript. For each benchmark and backbone, unlabeled calibration queries disjoint from all evaluation and development questions estimate the median and IQR of Eq.~\eqref{eq:norm} and the ECDFs of the retriever scores. The calibration reserves hold 500 queries for OK-VQA and HybridQA and 100 for VideoRAG-Synth, and WebQA uses training queries. The shared settings are $k=5$ and $\delta=0.08$, which is set a priori. The thresholds $\tau$ and $\taub$, the entropy weight $\beta$, and the strength $\gamma$ are frozen on development questions as described below; their selected values are $\tau=$~\ph{}, $\taub=$~\ph{}, $\beta=$~\ph{}, and $\gamma=$~\ph{} (Appendix~\ref{app:protocol}).

\textit{Development protocol.} Before any evaluation run, we fixed the development protocol: 256 development questions per benchmark (50 for VideoRAG-Synth), disjoint from calibration and evaluation questions, and the rules that map the development results to the remaining choices. These rules are written in \texttt{docs/DEV\_DECISIONS.md} (dated 2026-10-10, before any development run) and executed by a script that writes the frozen choices. The choices are, in order: the EAB form (start row and bias form, by its mean gain over static concatenation with equal weight per dataset, and counted as effective only if a max-statistic bootstrap over the six candidates excludes zero); the uncertainty variant (by the mean Spearman correlation between the score and the error of direct answers); the OK-VQA retrieval corpus (by the gain of static concatenation over direct answering); and then $\gamma$, the pair $(\beta,\taub)$, and $\tau$, each taken from its grid by a rule stated in Appendix~\ref{app:protocol}. Evaluation sets are then run once with these choices frozen, and no number in this paper comes from development questions. The sensitivity of the controller to $\beta$, $\taub$, and $\tau$ is reported on the evaluation sets only as a description and is not used to choose any setting. Decoding is greedy, so each configuration is run once; random seeds only fix the evidence orderings of Section~\ref{sec:order}. Significance markers use two-sided paired bootstrap tests over questions (5{,}000 resamples; by source video on VideoRAG-Synth) between the controller and each baseline of the column. The strongest baseline of each column is the one with the highest mean score, as determined by the code, and the $p$-values are Holm-corrected over the comparisons of that column. Latency is the measured end-to-end wall-clock time per query of each arm, including the probe round, routing, retrieval lookup, encoding, prefill, and decoding; static concatenation excludes the probe round. It is averaged over all evaluation queries and measured on \tbd{[[GPU model and count]]} with identical batch size, decoding settings, cache policy, and warmup. Code, prompts, and evaluation scripts are available at \tbd{[[anonymous repository URL]]}.

\subsubsection{Dependence on the Emphasized Chunks}
\label{sec:causal}
Attention alignment and citations do not show that answers depend on the emphasized chunks, so we intervene on \webimg{} with the retrieval decision fixed (Table~\ref{tab:causal}). Among queries where retrieval triggers and EAB is active, and where the gold answer string occurs in a retrieved text chunk, the target chunk is the highest-relevance retrieved text chunk that contains the answer; it is not necessarily the chunk with the largest bias. We mask the target chunk at the attention level, replace all of its gold aliases with a same-type entity, or add two distractors (controls and definitions in Appendix~\ref{app:protocol}). Masking changes \ph\% of EAB answers against \ph\% under static concatenation. EAB follows counterfactual edits in \ph\% of cases against \ph\% for static concatenation; a high follow rate would mean reliance on the target chunk, which also implies that a wrong target can mislead it. \section{Conclusion}
We present \method{}, a training-free latent-space controller for multimodal Web answering: an uncertainty score on evidence spans decides whether to retrieve, modality-level attention decides where, and EAB reuses the score as a bounded relevance prior. We compare \method{} with M2-RAG, SeaKR, and UniversalRAG on four benchmarks spanning text, image, table, and video evidence, and we measure the contributions of the retrieval decision and of EAB. Appendix~\ref{app:limitations} discusses limitations and future work.
