\section{Experiments}
\label{sec:experiments}
The experiments address four research questions. \textbf{RQ1}: Does \method{} improve answer quality on Web and other evidence formats? \textbf{RQ2}: How do the trigger, score normalization, modality routing, and EAB each contribute? \textbf{RQ3}: Does EAB reduce order sensitivity, improve citation support, and make answers depend on the emphasized chunks? \textbf{RQ4}: How robust is the controller to retrieval errors, calibration, and the backbone, and what does it cost? RQ2 examines Challenge~1 through the trigger, normalization, and routing controls, and RQ3 examines Challenge~2.

\subsection{Experimental Setup}
\label{sec:setup}
\textit{Datasets and evidence.} WebQA~\cite{chang2022webqa} is our primary Web task: its textual questions require facts retrieved from a pool of about 390k images and 540k text snippets, each of which is one chunk. We evaluate all 4{,}966 validation questions and report separately the 2{,}511 questions whose gold facts are images (\webimg{}) and the 2{,}455 whose gold facts are text snippets. Three further benchmarks vary the evidence format. OK-VQA~\cite{marino2019okvqa} tests image-based knowledge with passages retrieved from the GS-112K Google Search corpus~\cite{luo2021weakly} (168,306 sentences), the corpus commonly used for this benchmark. HybridQA~\cite{chen2020hybridqa} asks multi-hop questions over Web tables and the passages linked from their cells. VideoRAG-Synth asks about instructional procedures shown in HowTo100M videos~\cite{miech2019howto100m}, following VideoRAG~\cite{jeong2025videorag}; each retrieved video or clip is one chunk of sampled frames and its transcript. Only OK-VQA supplies context, the question image; the other benchmarks start cold, with text and image candidates on WebQA, table and text on HybridQA, and whole videos and clips on VideoRAG-Synth (2{,}000 HybridQA and 374 VideoRAG-Synth questions). For HybridQA and VideoRAG-Synth we use the query sets and unified corpora of the UniversalRAG suite~\cite{yeo2026universalrag}, and every method reads the same candidates, the top five per modality returned by the suite's own retrievers (Appendix~\ref{app:protocol}). Appendix~\ref{app:protocol} gives span lengths and score ranges per modality.

\textit{Evaluation metrics.} WebQA scores separate answer accuracy from BARTScore-based fluency (Appendix~\ref{app:webqa_scoring}); OK-VQA uses the official VQA accuracy; HybridQA and VideoRAG-Synth use the metrics of the UniversalRAG suite, exact match (EM) and ROUGE-L, with F1 and BERTScore in Appendix~\ref{app:supp}. Relative improvement over a baseline is $(\mathrm{ours}-\mathrm{baseline})/\mathrm{baseline}\times100\%$; all other differences are absolute points. For order sensitivity, we fix the retrieved chunks of each question and apply 12 orderings of the five retrieval ranks, drawn with three seeds, identically to all questions. We report the standard deviation of the score, the gap between relevance-sorted and reverse-sorted packing, and the flip rate, the share of questions whose correctness changes. We also report the attention--relevance divergence (ARD); attribution precision (AP) and Faith of citations, checked by alias matching and natural language inference (NLI); AURC and the Spearman $\rho$ between uncertainty and error; and the wrong-route (WR) and coverage (Cov.) rates of routing (definitions in Appendix~\ref{app:protocol}).

\textit{Baselines.} The baselines separate gains from retrieving evidence, from deciding when and where to retrieve, and from changing how the retrieved chunks are used. (1) Direct answering without retrieval. (2) Evidence selection and use in our pipeline: always-retrieve static concatenation; a cross-encoder, bge-reranker-v2-m3, built on BGE-M3~\cite{chen2024m3}, that selects the chunks for static concatenation; and Found-in-the-Middle attention calibration~\cite{hsieh2024found} on the static input. (3) Multimodal RAG: M2-RAG~\cite{ma2025m2rag} with its official single-stage summarizer over the same chunks, with tables serialized as text; it does not accept video. (4) Adaptive retrieval: SeaKR~\cite{yao2025seakr}, which reads internal states, with its threshold set on the calibration queries to the retrieval rate of $\Uomni$. (5) Modality routing: UniversalRAG~\cite{yeo2026universalrag}, whose official router, trained after removing our evaluation questions from its training data, decides from the query whether and where to retrieve. Next-token entropy (NTE) as the trigger and a PASTA-inspired uniform bias~\cite{zhang2024pasta} are ablations of our controller (Section~\ref{sec:ablation}). All methods share the candidate pools, the backbone, the prompt format, decoding limits, at most $k=5$ retrieved chunks, truncation rules, and answer-normalization scripts, and each question is answered by every method from the same packed input; baselines receive \tbd{[[tuning budget]]}.

\textit{Prompts and answer format.} All configurations receive identical instructions, and every chunk carries an identifier such as \texttt{[E3]} for citation. OK-VQA asks for a word or phrase, WebQA for one sentence, and HybridQA and VideoRAG-Synth keep the answer instructions of the UniversalRAG suite (the exact answer, and a complete sentence). The first answer token alone determines \ph\% of OK-VQA, \ph\% of HybridQA, \ph\% of VideoRAG-Synth, and \ph\% of WebQA answers, which is why EAB starts at the row that predicts it (Section~\ref{sec:eab}).

\textit{Implementation and calibration.} Qwen3-VL-8B-Instruct~\cite{bai2025qwen3vl} is the primary backbone, with InternVL3.5-8B~\cite{wang2025internvl35} for transfer. Text and image retrieval use BGE-large-en-v1.5~\cite{xiao2024cpack} and CLIP ViT-L/14~\cite{radford2021clip} with FAISS~\cite{johnson2021faiss}; on HybridQA and VideoRAG-Synth these encoders score the shared candidates to obtain $r_j$. Tables are serialized row by row, and each retrieved video or clip is represented by 8 uniformly sampled frames with its transcript. For each benchmark and backbone, 500 unlabeled queries disjoint from all evaluation questions estimate the median and IQR for Eq.~\eqref{eq:norm} and the ECDFs of the retriever scores; these queries come from the training split of WebQA, from a held-out slice of the OK-VQA validation questions, and, for HybridQA and VideoRAG-Synth, whose suite has no training queries, from a held-out slice of the query set. Shared hyperparameters are $(\tau,\taub,\beta,\gamma,\delta)=(0.35,0.40,0.30,1.20,0.08)$ (Appendix~\ref{app:protocol}).

\textit{Development protocol.} Before any evaluation run, we fixed on 256 development questions per benchmark, disjoint from calibration and evaluation questions, the remaining design choices by rules stated in advance and released with the code: one EAB form for all benchmarks (start row and bias form, by the largest same-input gain over static concatenation averaged across benchmarks), the uncertainty variant (by the Spearman correlation between the score and the error of direct answers), and the OK-VQA retrieval corpus (by the gain of static concatenation over direct answering). Evaluation sets are then run once with these choices frozen, and no number in this paper comes from development questions. Decoding is greedy, so each configuration is run once; random seeds only fix the evidence orderings of Section~\ref{sec:order}. Significance markers use paired bootstrap tests over questions (5{,}000 resamples, by source video on VideoRAG-Synth) against the strongest baseline ($p<0.05$). Latency covers uncertainty scoring, retrieval, evidence preparation, and generation, averaged over all queries, and is measured for all methods on \tbd{[[GPU model and count]]} with identical batch size, decoding settings, cache policy, and warmup. Code, prompts, and evaluation scripts are available at \tbd{[[anonymous repository URL]]}.

\subsubsection{Dependence on the Emphasized Chunks}
\label{sec:causal}
Attention alignment and citations do not show that answers depend on the emphasized chunks, so we intervene on \webimg{} with the retrieval decision fixed (Table~\ref{tab:causal}): where EAB is active and a retrieved text chunk contains the gold answer, we mask that chunk, replace its answer with a same-type entity, or add two distractors (controls in Appendix~\ref{app:protocol}). \ai{Masking the target chunk changes \ph\% of EAB answers against \ph\% under static concatenation, and EAB follows counterfactual edits more often (\ph\% against \ph\%), so its answers rely more on the target chunk, which also means that a wrong target misleads it more often. \section{Conclusion}
We present \method{}, a training-free latent-space controller for multimodal Web answering: an uncertainty score on evidence spans decides whether to retrieve, modality-level attention decides where, and EAB reuses the score as a bounded relevance prior. \ai{\method{} outperforms M2-RAG, SeaKR, and UniversalRAG on four benchmarks spanning text, image, table, and video evidence}, with gains from both the retrieval decision and EAB. Appendix~\ref{app:limitations} discusses limitations and future work.
