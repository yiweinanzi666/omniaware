\title[Risk-Controlled Retrieval Arbitration]{Omni-Aware: Risk-Controlled Retrieval Arbitration across Modalities}

\author{Anonymous Author(s)}
\affiliation{\institution{Anonymous Institution}\country{}}

\begin{abstract}
Multimodal Web question answering has to choose, for each question, whether to trust parametric knowledge or evidence from text, from images, or from both.
\method{} v2 decodes five actions in one run under one frozen prompt: direct answering, static text, static image, static mixed evidence, and reranked static evidence.
It reads first-token entropy and entropy divided by $\log|V|$, the top-1--top-2 logit margin, the greedy sequence log-probability and the generated length, the KL divergence between an evidence action's first-token distribution and the direct distribution, and the retriever's top-1 score and top-1--top-2 gap.
A rule with no fitted weights (v2-Rule) and one logistic regression with seven parameters (v2-Cal: an intercept and six features) select an action.
Thresholds are screened by fixed-sequence Learn-then-Test with $\alpha=\delta=0.10$.
The loss is 1 when the chosen action scores below the strongest single action.
With probability at least $1-\delta$ over the calibration draw, every accepted threshold has true degradation rate at most $\delta$.
The claim does not say that the method is never worse.
Utility, not accuracy, selects the threshold inside the accepted set.
The strongest single action is chosen on the same calibration sample, which is slightly anti-conservative.
The primary relative gain uses that action as the denominator.
Gain versus static mixed evidence, and a per-question oracle, are reported as well.
OK-VQA uses official VQA accuracy only.
WebQA's primary metric is acc$_{\mathrm{approx}}$, together with normalized BART and QA$_{\mathrm{approx}}$ on image and text subsets.
Evidence Attention Bias is not a main-method action.
The v2 evaluation has not been run; every new experimental number in this draft is a placeholder.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

Web retrieval-augmented generation answers a question by retrieving evidence and conditioning a generator on it~\cite{guu2020realm,lewis2020rag}.
On the Web, the supporting facts may sit in a text passage, an image, or both, and the same question can be helped by one source and hurt by another.
The decision studied here is which action to take: answer from the model alone, from text evidence, from image evidence, from mixed evidence, or from a reranked mixed list.
Attention-level steering is a poor substitute for that decision.
A bias can move attention onto a supporting span while the first answer token, and therefore the answer, stays where it was.
Section~\ref{sec:steer} quotes that measurement from the project root-cause report dated 13~October~2026.
It is an analysis of the previous pipeline, not a result of the v2 runs, which have not been executed.

Format and metric choices can move scores by more than the gap between systems.
The evaluation therefore freezes one answer prompt for every action, scores OK-VQA with official VQA accuracy only, and scores WebQA primarily with acc$_{\mathrm{approx}}$, reporting normalized BART and QA$_{\mathrm{approx}}$ on image and text subsets beside it.
The primary relative gain uses, as its denominator, the single action with the best score on the calibration split.
Historical cached baselines are not used.

Our contributions are as follows:
\begin{itemize}
  \item A measurement, under that protocol, of when retrieval helps or hurts on WebQA and OK-VQA, including image and text subsets and the gap between the best single action and the per-question oracle.
  The new measurement has not been run.
  Its cells, including that gap, are \ph{}.
  \item v2-Rule, which fits no weights, and v2-Cal, one logistic regression with seven parameters, which choose among the five actions using the signals in Section~\ref{sec:signals}.
  Fixed-sequence Learn-then-Test at $\alpha=\delta=0.10$ accepts a threshold only under the degradation-rate claim in Section~\ref{sec:risk}.
  Utility, not accuracy, selects the threshold inside the accepted set.
  The claim does not say that the method is never worse.
  \item A mechanism analysis, titled as the subsection in Section~\ref{sec:steer}, that attention steering moves attention and does not move answers.
  Every number in that subsection is copied from the 13~October~2026 root-cause report and is attributed there.
\end{itemize}

\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:related}

\textit{Web and multimodal retrieval-augmented generation.}
Web answer engines retrieve and cite pages at query time~\cite{nakano2021webgpt,liu2023webglm}, and WebQA poses multimodal Web answering over text and image facts~\cite{chang2022webqa}.
Multimodal RAG systems~\cite{abootorabi2025survey} retrieve images, knowledge memories, or visual documents~\cite{adjali2024multilevel,chen2022murag,hu2023reveal,wang2025vidorag,yu2025visrag}, generate multimodal answers~\cite{ma2025m2rag,wu2025visualrag}, or refine selection by reranking, reflection, or adaptive visual selection~\cite{chen2025ragvl,zhai2025samrag,zhang2024mr2ag}.
They improve which chunks are retrieved and how each format is encoded.
We keep those format-specific retrievers and encoders.
The controller in this paper chooses among the five actions in Section~\ref{sec:problem}.

\textit{Adaptive evidence acquisition.}
Adaptive retrieval decides from the generation state whether external knowledge is needed: Self-RAG learns reflection signals~\cite{asai2024selfrag}, FLARE retrieves when upcoming tokens have low probability~\cite{jiang2023flare}, DRAGIN combines token entropy with attention to decide when and what to retrieve~\cite{su2024dragin}, Adaptive-RAG classifies question complexity~\cite{jeong2024adaptiverag}, and SeaKR measures the consistency of sampled hidden states~\cite{yao2025seakr}; their cost--quality tradeoffs have been compared~\cite{moskvoretskii2025adaptive}.
For multimodal inputs, MMA-RAG classifies hidden states of the input and the retrieved visual context~\cite{du2026mmarag}, and UniversalRAG routes the query alone to modality- and granularity-specific corpora~\cite{yeo2026universalrag}; retriever signals do not reliably improve such query-only routers~\cite{li2026beyond}.
These methods read generated text, the query, retriever scores, or pooled hidden states, and most train a router or classifier or sample many generations.
v2 reads first-token and retriever signals from one shared decoding of a fixed action set, fits at most the seven-parameter regression in Section~\ref{sec:cal}, and screens thresholds with the procedure in Section~\ref{sec:risk}.

\textit{Knowledge conflict and inference-time evidence control.}
Retrieved context can agree or conflict with parametric knowledge, which is why the action set contains a direct arm and separate text, image, and mixed arms.
Position sensitivity~\cite{liu2024lost} has motivated multi-scale positional encoding~\cite{zhang2024mspoe} and positional attention calibration~\cite{hsieh2024found}; text-only RAG readers are less affected than long-context models~\cite{cuconasu2025positional}, and multimodal readers can prefer early passages, while reranking does not by itself remove that preference~\cite{liu2026lostend}.
OpenDecoder multiplies attention scores by retrieval features and fine-tunes the decoder~\cite{mo2026opendecoder}; PASTA emphasizes user-specified spans on profiled heads~\cite{zhang2024pasta}, which at one attention row equals a constant span bias; PAI amplifies attention to image tokens~\cite{liu2024pai}; and MAD-RAG restores attention to question-relevant image regions that retrieved text draws away~\cite{zhao2026madrag}.
Section~\ref{sec:steer} quotes a measurement in which an attention bias moves attention onto supporting evidence while the answer stays put.
Evidence Attention Bias is confined to that analysis and is not a main-method action.

\section{Problem Definition}
\label{sec:problem}

An instance is a query $q$, optional context such as the OK-VQA question image, and an evidence pool.
A frozen generator produces an answer string.
The main prompt is shared by every action.
It asks for the frozen answer format and does not instruct the model to emit a sources line in place of an answer.
WebQA answers are one sentence aligned with the reference sentence style.
OK-VQA answers are at most three words after official VQA normalization.
That format is chosen on the development split and then frozen.

The action set is
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
\mathcal{A}=\{\texttt{direct},\ &\texttt{static\_text},\ \texttt{static\_image},\\
&\texttt{static\_mixed},\ \texttt{rerank\_static}\}.
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:actions}
\end{equation}
\texttt{direct} uses no retrieved evidence.
The three static actions pack text, image, or mixed evidence.
\texttt{rerank\_static} packs the mixed list after a reranker.
Evidence Attention Bias is not a member of $\mathcal{A}$.
All five actions are decoded in the same run, on the same candidates, with the same prompt and the same decoding budget.

The selector returns one action $a\in\mathcal{A}$.
Let $s(\cdot)$ be the official metric on a given split, and let $a^{\star}$ be the single action in $\mathcal{A}$ with the highest score on the calibration split.
The primary relative gain on a later split uses $s(a^{\star})$ as the denominator.
The same tables also report the gain versus \texttt{static\_mixed}.
The oracle picks, for each question, the action in $\mathcal{A}$ with the best score.
Risk control is stated in Section~\ref{sec:risk}.
Its claim is a bound on the degradation rate of accepted thresholds.
It does not say that the selector is never worse than $a^{\star}$.

\section{Method}
\label{sec:method}

\subsection{Signals}
\label{sec:signals}
Each action stores the entropy of its first-token distribution and the same entropy divided by $\log|V|$, the gap between the top-1 and top-2 logits, the log-probability of the greedy answer and the number of generated tokens, and, for an evidence action, the KL divergence between that action's first-token distribution and the direct action's first-token distribution.
The run also stores the retriever's top-1 score and the gap between retriever top-1 and top-2, per modality.
\texttt{direct} has no retrieved list: its KL and its retriever features are defined as zero.
\texttt{gold-in-top-$k$} is computed only for offline analysis.
It is not an inference-time feature and it is not an input to v2-Cal.

One direct prefill produces the direct first-token signals.
Each evidence action then uses a short greedy decode.
A cheaper variant, reported separately, decodes evidence actions only when the direct confidence is below a threshold.
Its measured cost is \ph{}, because that variant has not been run.

\subsection{v2-Rule}
\label{sec:rule}
v2-Rule fits no weights.
If the direct action's confidence, read from the logit margin or from the normalized first-token entropy, is above a threshold $\tau_1$, the rule selects \texttt{direct}.
Otherwise it selects the evidence action with the highest confidence whose answer has token F1 at least $\theta$ with at least one other action.
The thresholds are chosen on the calibration split.
The selection criterion is utility, defined in Section~\ref{sec:risk}, and not the threshold with the highest accuracy.

\subsection{v2-Cal}
\label{sec:cal}
v2-Cal is one logistic regression with seven parameters: an intercept and six coefficients.
The six features are the top-1--top-2 logit margin, normalized first-token entropy (entropy$/\!\log|V|$), the greedy sequence log-probability divided by the generated length, the KL divergence against \texttt{direct}, the token F1 between that action's answer and the direct answer, and the retriever top-1 score.
Unnormalized entropy, generated length as its own coordinate, and the retriever top-1--top-2 gap are stored in the run record and do not receive extra coefficients.
The same weight vector is applied to each action's feature vector.
The selected action is the one with the highest predicted probability of being correct under the official metric.
The regression is fit on the calibration split.
We describe it as calibration, not as a trained router.
A previous OK-VQA rule (\texttt{original\_retrieve} or $p_{\mathrm{image}}<0.5$) used a trained query classifier.
v2 does not use that classifier.

\subsection{Risk Control}
\label{sec:risk}
Thresholds are screened by fixed-sequence Learn-then-Test.
The loss is 1 when the chosen action scores below the strongest single action:
\begin{equation}
L(a)=\mathbf{1}\{s(a)<s(a^{\star})\},\qquad
u(a)=s(a)-s(a^{\star}).
\label{eq:loss}
\end{equation}
Here $\alpha=\delta=0.10$.
The claim is: with probability at least $1-\delta$ over the calibration draw, every accepted threshold has true degradation rate at most $\delta$.
It does not claim the method is never worse.
Utility, not accuracy, selects the threshold inside the accepted set.
The best single action is chosen on the same calibration sample, which is slightly anti-conservative.
v2-Rule uses this utility to choose its thresholds.
v2-Cal is reported as its own arm.
Any threshold attached to it is chosen by the same rule, on the calibration split, and is not searched on the test split.

\subsection{Cost}
\label{sec:cost}
In the worst case each question uses one direct prefill and one short decode per action in $\mathcal{A}$.
The evaluation reports the mean number of generator calls, the number of input tokens, and the latency, next to always decoding \texttt{static\_mixed} and next to SeaKR~\cite{yao2025seakr}.
Those measurements are \ph{}.
The GPU-time figures in the repository README are planning estimates, not measurements, and they are not copied into the tables.
